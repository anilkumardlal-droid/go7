About This Website

This website provides a central place for information about domain names. Visitors may learn about available domains, submit inquiries, discuss pricing and, where applicable, proceed with a domain transaction.

Visitors may arrive at this website through multiple domain names. When this happens, information about the domain used to reach this website may be retained so that an inquiry remains connected to the domain the visitor intended to contact us about.

Domains and Website Access

Visitors may arrive through another domain name that directs them to this website. For example, example.in may direct a visitor to this website. Where technically available, the source domain may be retained so that the inquiry can be associated with the intended domain.

Simple example: Domain A → This Website → Inquiry for Domain A.

Information We May Collect

Depending on how you use the website, we may collect:

  • Your name.
  • Your email address.
  • The subject and message of your inquiry.
  • The domain associated with your visit or inquiry (Source Domain).
  • Inquiry identification and submission date/time information.
  • Basic technical and security information such as IP address, country, city, region, device type, browser and timezone.

Please do not submit passwords, card numbers, authentication codes, confidential credentials or other sensitive information through the inquiry form unless specifically required and requested through a secure process.

Inquiry information may be used to understand your request, identify the relevant domain, communicate with you, discuss availability and price, and proceed with a potential domain transaction.

Technical information may be used for website security, abuse prevention, troubleshooting, rate limiting and maintaining reliability.

The contact form uses security controls designed to help prevent spam, automated abuse and malicious activity. A security verification service may process certain technical information to determine whether a request is legitimate.

Reasonable rate limits and automated checks may also be used to protect the website and its users.

How You Can Trust a Domain Transaction

Domain transactions should be handled carefully. Before proceeding, you should:

  • Confirm the exact spelling of the domain and its extension.
  • Confirm the final purchase price in writing.
  • Confirm any additional transaction or transfer fees.
  • Agree on the payment method and recipient.
  • Keep written records of important communications and agreed terms.
  • Verify the receiving registrar or account details before a transfer.
  • Do not proceed if payment instructions suddenly change without independent verification.
  • Never share passwords, OTPs, recovery codes or unnecessary credentials.

Important security rule: a legitimate domain transaction should not require you to provide your password or a one-time authentication code.

How a Domain Sale Can Work

Step 1 — Visitor arrives: A visitor reaches this website directly or through another domain that directs the visitor here.

Step 2 — Inquiry submitted: The visitor submits an inquiry with contact and request details.

Step 3 — Domain identified: The intended domain is identified, including the source domain where applicable.

Step 4 — Availability and price discussion: Availability, pricing and other relevant terms are discussed.

Step 5 — Terms confirmed: The exact domain, final amount, payment method, transfer procedure and material conditions are confirmed.

Step 6 — Payment: Payment is made using the agreed payment method.

Step 7 — Payment verification: Payment is verified as received or cleared, as applicable.

Step 8 — Domain transfer: The domain is transferred through the applicable registrar or registry process.

Step 9 — Completion and records: The transaction is completed and relevant records may be retained for legitimate business, security, accounting or legal purposes.

Pricing and Negotiation

There is no requirement that every domain be sold at the same price. Domain value may depend on factors such as length, memorability, extension, commercial usefulness, demand and the circumstances of the transaction.

An asking price, indicative price or initial offer is not a final transaction price unless it is expressly confirmed as final. Serious and reasonable offers are welcome. Before completion, the final domain, amount, payment method, transfer procedure and material conditions should be recorded clearly.

Payment and Transaction Details

Payment should only be made after the transaction details have been agreed. There is no single required payment method for every transaction.

Before sending money, obtain written confirmation of the exact domain, final price, recipient, payment method, transfer procedure and any additional fees.

Any change to a bank account, payment address, wallet, recipient or amount should be independently verified. A screenshot showing an initiated payment does not necessarily mean that payment has been received or cleared.

Domain transfer should take place after the applicable payment and transaction conditions have been satisfied.

Secure Escrow or Third-Party Transaction Services

A recognized third-party escrow or transaction service may be used if mutually agreed by the parties. Such services have their own terms, privacy policies, fees and verification procedures.

Not every transaction necessarily uses the same escrow or transaction provider.

Domain Transfer

After the agreed payment and other applicable conditions are satisfied, the domain may be transferred through the normal registrar or registry process.

A transfer may require account details, authorization or other information. The buyer should provide accurate receiving details and carefully check the spelling and destination before a transfer is initiated.

Registrar and registry rules may govern transfer procedures, restrictions and possible reversals.

Transaction Records

Emails, inquiry information, payment confirmations and transaction communications may be retained for legitimate business, accounting, dispute-resolution, security and legal purposes.

Fraud Prevention

Be cautious of unexpected payment accounts, requests for passwords or authentication codes, last-minute payment changes or unusual urgency.

Payment and transaction instructions should be verified through a trusted communication channel before proceeding.

Data Security

Reasonable technical and organizational measures are used to protect information. However, no transmission or storage method can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

Avoid providing sensitive credentials through website forms or ordinary email.

Data Retention

Inquiry and transaction-related information may be retained for as long as reasonably necessary for communication, business records, security, dispute resolution, accounting or legal requirements. Retention periods may depend on the type of information and the circumstances.
