Privacy Policy

Your privacy, security and trust are important to us. This policy explains what information may be collected when you visit this website, including when you arrive through another domain name that directs visitors to this website, how that information is used, and how domain inquiries, negotiations, payments and transfers are handled.

Effective Date: September 11, 2026 • Last Updated: September 11, 2026

1. About This Website

This website is operated as a central place where visitors can learn about domain names that may be available for sale and can contact us if they are interested in purchasing a particular domain.

The purpose of the website is straightforward: a visitor can arrive here, identify the domain that brought them to the website, review the available information, and send an inquiry to begin a genuine conversation about that domain.

The website may be reached through multiple domain names. Some domains may be used as the primary website address, while other domains owned or controlled by us may be configured to direct visitors here.

Because the same website can receive visitors from different domains, we may use source-domain information to keep the inquiry connected with the domain the visitor originally intended to visit.

The purpose of recording the source domain is practical and administrative. It does not create a different privacy policy for different visitors.

2. Domains and Website Access

Visitors may arrive at this website through another domain name that directs them here. In such cases, the visitor may still be interested in purchasing the domain they originally entered.

Arriving at this website through another domain does not change which domain the visitor may be interested in purchasing. The destination website is simply being used as the place where the inquiry and communication are handled.

Domain A → This Website → Inquiry for Domain A

To reduce confusion, the original domain can be passed to the website as source information. When an inquiry is submitted, that source information may be stored with the inquiry so that we know which domain the visitor was referring to.

This is especially useful when several domains are being marketed through the same website.

3. Information We May Collect

When you voluntarily submit an inquiry, we may collect information necessary to communicate with you and process the inquiry, such as:

  • Your name.
  • Your email address.
  • The subject and message of your inquiry.
  • The domain associated with your visit or inquiry (Source Domain).
  • Inquiry identification and submission date/time information.
  • Basic technical and security information such as IP address, country, city, region, device type, browser and timezone.

We do not ask you to provide unnecessary sensitive personal information in the inquiry form. Please do not submit passwords, payment-card numbers, authentication codes, OTPs, recovery codes or other confidential credentials through the contact form.

4. How Inquiry Information Is Used

Information submitted through the inquiry form is used primarily to understand your request, identify the domain you are asking about, communicate with you, discuss availability and price, and, where applicable, proceed with a domain transaction.

Technical information may also be used for security, abuse prevention, troubleshooting, rate limiting and maintaining the reliability of the website.

5. Security and Anti-Spam Protection

The contact form uses security controls designed to reduce automated abuse, spam and fraudulent submissions. A security verification service may process technical information required to determine whether a submission appears to come from a genuine visitor.

Requests may also be subject to reasonable rate limits and automated checks. These measures are intended to protect the website, its owner and genuine visitors from abuse.

6. Information Sharing

We do not sell your personal information to advertisers or unrelated parties.

Information may be processed by service providers that are necessary to operate the website, protect it from abuse, store inquiry records, or deliver email communications.

  • Website hosting providers.
  • Security and anti-abuse services.
  • Email delivery providers.
  • Database or inquiry storage providers.
  • Domain registration or transfer providers.
  • Payment or transaction providers.
  • Escrow providers where applicable.

Information may also be disclosed when reasonably necessary to comply with applicable law, respond to a lawful request, protect the website or prevent fraud, abuse or unauthorized activity.

7. Cookies and Technical Information

The website may use essential browser storage, cookies or similar technical mechanisms where necessary for functionality, security, session management or website operation.

Basic server-side information may also be available through hosting, security and infrastructure services.

The exact information available can depend on your browser, device and the security services used to deliver the website.

8. Domain Transaction and Security

Buying a domain name is a real transfer of a digital asset, so both the buyer and seller should take reasonable steps to make the transaction clear and verifiable.

Before paying, a buyer should independently check the exact domain name, confirm who they are communicating with, confirm the agreed amount, and understand how the domain will be transferred.

If anything changes at the last minute—especially the payment account, payment amount, or transfer instructions—the buyer should pause and verify the change before proceeding.

  • Confirm the exact spelling and extension of the domain.
  • Confirm the final purchase price in writing before payment.
  • Confirm whether any additional transaction or transfer fees apply.
  • Agree on the payment method and payment recipient before sending funds.
  • Keep written records of important messages and confirmations.
  • Verify the receiving registrar or account details before transfer.
  • Do not proceed if payment instructions suddenly change without verification.
  • Never share passwords, OTPs, recovery codes or unnecessary account credentials.

A legitimate domain transaction should not require you to disclose your password or one-time authentication code to another person. If someone asks for such information, stop the transaction and verify the request independently.

9. How a Domain Sale Can Work

Every domain transaction can have slightly different circumstances, but the general process is intended to be simple and transparent.

Step 1 — Visitor arrives

A visitor reaches the website either directly or through a domain that has been configured to direct visitors to this website. If the visitor came through another domain, the source-domain information may be retained so the inquiry can be associated with the correct domain.

Step 2 — Inquiry is submitted

The visitor enters their name, email address and message and submits the contact form.

Step 3 — Domain is identified

The source domain and inquiry details are reviewed so that the intended domain is clear.

Step 4 — Availability and price discussion

We communicate with the interested buyer about whether the domain is available, the asking price or offer, and any relevant conditions.

Step 5 — Terms are confirmed

Before payment, the buyer and seller should have a clear written understanding of the exact domain, final amount, payment method, transfer procedure and any other important conditions.

Step 6 — Payment

The buyer makes payment using the payment method agreed for that particular transaction.

Step 7 — Payment verification

The transaction status is checked. The transfer should not be treated as complete merely because the buyer has sent a screenshot or initiated a payment.

Step 8 — Domain transfer

Once the agreed conditions are satisfied, the domain transfer is initiated through the relevant registrar or standard domain-transfer process.

Step 9 — Completion and records

After the transfer process has been completed or the agreed transaction has otherwise been concluded, both sides should retain their relevant communication and transaction records.

10. Pricing and Negotiation

There is no requirement for every domain to have the same price. The value of a domain can depend on factors such as its length, memorability, extension, commercial usefulness, demand and the circumstances of the particular transaction.

An asking price, an indicative price, a visitor's initial offer, or a number mentioned during an early conversation should not be understood as a final transaction price unless the final amount is expressly confirmed between the buyer and seller.

Visitors are welcome to submit serious and reasonable offers. Negotiation may take place by email or another agreed communication channel.

11. Payment and Transaction Details

Payment is handled only after the important transaction details have been discussed and agreed.

There is no single payment method that must be used for every domain sale. The appropriate method can depend on the buyer, seller, transaction value, practical requirements and any mutually agreed third-party service.

Before sending money, the buyer should have written confirmation of at least the following:

  • The exact domain name being purchased.
  • The final agreed price.
  • The payment recipient.
  • The payment method.
  • The basic domain-transfer procedure.
  • Any additional fees.

If an email suddenly provides a different bank account, payment address, wallet, recipient name or amount from what was previously confirmed, do not assume the new information is genuine. Pause and request confirmation through a trusted communication channel.

A screenshot showing that a payment was initiated may not by itself mean that the payment has been received or cleared.

12. Secure Escrow or Third-Party Transaction Services

Where mutually agreed or appropriate, a recognized third-party escrow or transaction service may be used.

If such a service is selected, its own terms, privacy policy, fees and verification requirements will apply to that part of the transaction.

We do not claim that every transaction will use the same payment or escrow provider. The method should be agreed before payment.

13. Domain Transfer

After the agreed payment and transaction conditions have been satisfied, the domain is transferred using the normal procedure supported by the relevant registrar and registry.

The exact steps can vary depending on the domain extension, registrar and account setup.

A transfer may require the buyer to provide a correct registrar account, receiving account information, authorization details, or other information required by the registrar.

The buyer is responsible for providing accurate receiving details and completing any actions that the registrar requires on their side.

Before the transfer is initiated, the buyer should carefully check the spelling of the domain and the destination account.

14. Transaction Records

Relevant emails, inquiry information, payment confirmations and transaction communications may be retained for legitimate business, accounting, dispute resolution, security or legal purposes, subject to applicable requirements.

Keeping written records helps both parties understand what domain was discussed, what price was agreed and what transaction procedure was selected.

15. Fraud Prevention

Please be cautious of anyone asking you to pay to an unexpected account, requesting passwords or authentication codes, changing payment details at the last moment, or creating unusual urgency.

If payment instructions appear different from previously confirmed details, verify them through a trusted communication channel before sending funds.

16. Data Security

Reasonable technical and organizational measures are used to protect inquiry information against unauthorized access, misuse or loss.

However, no internet transmission or storage system can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

Visitors should therefore avoid submitting confidential credentials, financial account passwords, authentication codes or other highly sensitive information through ordinary website forms or email.

17. Data Retention

Inquiry and transaction-related information may be retained for as long as reasonably necessary for communication, business records, security, dispute handling, accounting or legal obligations.

Retention periods can depend on the type of information and circumstances of the interaction.

Requests may need to be reasonably verified before information is disclosed or changed.

For a domain purchase, it is especially important that the exact domain name, final amount, payment recipient and transfer process are clear before money is sent.
