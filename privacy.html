About This Website

This website is operated as a central place where visitors can learn about domain names that may be available for sale and can contact us if they are interested in purchasing a particular domain. The purpose of the website is straightforward: a visitor can arrive here, identify the domain that brought them to the website, review the available information, and send an inquiry to begin a genuine conversation about that domain.

The website may be reached through multiple domain names. Some domains may be used as the primary website address, while other domains owned or controlled by us may be configured to direct visitors here. Because the same website can receive visitors from different domains, we use the source-domain information to keep the inquiry connected with the domain the visitor originally intended to visit.

This is important in a multi-domain setup. If someone enters a domain directly, follows a link, or reaches the website through another domain, the inquiry should still clearly identify the domain being discussed. The purpose of recording the source domain is therefore practical and administrative, not to create a different privacy policy for different visitors.

Domains and Website Access

Visitors may arrive at this website through another domain name that directs them here. In such cases, the visitor may still be interested in purchasing the domain they originally entered.

Arriving at this website through another domain does not change which domain the visitor may be interested in purchasing. For example, if a visitor enters example.in and that domain directs the visitor to this website, the visitor may still be contacting us specifically about example.in. The destination website is simply being used as the place where the inquiry and communication are handled.

To reduce confusion, the original domain can be passed to the website as source information. When an inquiry is submitted, that source information can be stored with the inquiry so that we know which domain the visitor was referring to. This is especially useful when several domains are being marketed through the same website.

Simple example: Domain A → This Website → Inquiry for Domain A. The website is the common communication point, while Domain A remains the subject of the inquiry.

How You Can Trust a Domain Transaction

Buying a domain name is a real transfer of a digital asset, so both the buyer and seller should take reasonable steps to make the transaction clear and verifiable. Trust should not depend only on a website, an email address, or a verbal promise. The safest approach is to keep the important details written down and confirm them before money is sent.

Before paying, a buyer should independently check the exact domain name, confirm who they are communicating with, confirm the agreed amount, and understand how the domain will be transferred. If anything changes at the last minute—especially the payment account, payment amount, or transfer instructions—the buyer should pause and verify the change before proceeding.

We also recommend keeping the complete email conversation and any written confirmation relating to the domain. These records can make it much easier for both parties to understand what was agreed if there is ever a misunderstanding.

  • Confirm the exact spelling and extension of the domain.
  • Confirm the final purchase price in writing before payment.
  • Confirm whether any additional transaction or transfer fees apply.
  • Agree on the payment method and payment recipient before sending funds.
  • Keep written records of important messages and confirmations.
  • Verify the receiving registrar or account details before transfer.
  • Do not proceed if payment instructions suddenly change without verification.
  • Never share passwords, OTPs, recovery codes or unnecessary account credentials.

Important security rule: A legitimate domain transaction should not require you to disclose your password or one-time authentication code to another person. If someone asks for such information, stop the transaction and verify the request independently.

How a Domain Sale Can Work

Every domain transaction can have slightly different circumstances, but the general process is intended to be simple and transparent. The following describes how a normal transaction may proceed from the first inquiry through completion.

Step 1 — Visitor arrives: A visitor reaches the website either directly or through a domain that has been configured to direct visitors to this website. If the visitor came through another domain, the source-domain information may be retained so the inquiry can be associated with the correct domain.
Step 2 — Inquiry is submitted: The visitor enters their name, email address and message and submits the contact form. The inquiry is received for review and communication.
Step 3 — Domain is identified: The source domain and inquiry details are reviewed so that the intended domain is clear. If there is any uncertainty, the domain can be confirmed with the buyer before discussing payment.
Step 4 — Availability and price discussion: We communicate with the interested buyer about whether the domain is available, the asking price or offer, and any relevant conditions. A conversation or preliminary figure should not be treated as final unless the final terms are confirmed.
Step 5 — Terms are confirmed: Before payment, the buyer and seller should have a clear written understanding of the exact domain, final amount, payment method, transfer procedure and any other important conditions.
Step 6 — Payment: The buyer makes payment using the payment method agreed for that particular transaction. Depending on the circumstances, this may be a direct payment arrangement or another mutually agreed transaction method.
Step 7 — Payment verification: The transaction status is checked. The transfer should not be treated as complete merely because the buyer has sent a screenshot or initiated a payment; the applicable payment conditions should be satisfied and verified.
Step 8 — Domain transfer: Once the agreed conditions are satisfied, the domain transfer is initiated through the relevant registrar or standard domain-transfer process. The buyer should provide accurate receiving-account information and complete any required authorization steps.
Step 9 — Completion and records: After the transfer process has been completed or the agreed transaction has otherwise been concluded, both sides should retain their relevant communication and transaction records.

Pricing and Negotiation

There is no requirement for every domain to have the same price. The value of a domain can depend on factors such as its length, memorability, extension, commercial usefulness, demand and the circumstances of the particular transaction. For that reason, pricing can vary from one domain to another.

An asking price, an indicative price, a visitor's initial offer, or a number mentioned during an early conversation should not be understood as a final transaction price unless the final amount is expressly confirmed between the buyer and seller.

Visitors are welcome to submit serious and reasonable offers. Negotiation may take place by email or another agreed communication channel. Once both sides agree, the final domain, purchase amount, payment method, transfer procedure and other material conditions should be recorded clearly before payment is made.

Payment and Transaction Details

Payment is handled only after the important transaction details have been discussed and agreed. There is no single payment method that must be used for every domain sale. The appropriate method can depend on the buyer, seller, transaction value, practical requirements and any mutually agreed third-party service.

Before sending money, the buyer should have a written confirmation of at least the following: the exact domain name being purchased, the final agreed price, the payment recipient, the payment method, and the basic domain-transfer procedure. If there are any additional fees, those should also be understood before payment.

The buyer should verify payment instructions carefully. If an email suddenly provides a different bank account, payment address, wallet, recipient name or amount from what was previously confirmed, do not assume the new information is genuine. Pause and request confirmation through a trusted communication channel.

A screenshot showing that a payment was initiated may not by itself mean that the payment has been received or cleared. Where necessary, payment status may be verified before the domain transfer is completed.

Once the applicable payment conditions have been satisfied, the domain transfer can proceed according to the agreed procedure. The final completion of the transaction should be understood from the written terms and the actual transfer/payment status, rather than from an informal assumption.

Secure Escrow or Third-Party Transaction Services

Where mutually agreed or appropriate, a recognized third-party escrow or transaction service may be used. If such a service is selected, its own terms, privacy policy, fees and verification requirements will apply to that part of the transaction.

We do not claim that every transaction will use the same payment or escrow provider. The method should be agreed before payment.

Domain Transfer

After the agreed payment and transaction conditions have been satisfied, the domain is transferred using the normal procedure supported by the relevant registrar and registry. The exact steps can vary depending on the domain extension, registrar and account setup.

A transfer may require the buyer to provide a correct registrar account, receiving account information, authorization details, or other information required by the registrar. The buyer is responsible for providing accurate receiving details and completing any actions that the registrar requires on their side.

Before the transfer is initiated, the buyer should carefully check the spelling of the domain and the destination account. A domain sent to an incorrect account can create avoidable complications. Once a transfer has been initiated or completed, registrar or registry rules may determine whether and how the action can be reversed.

The transfer process should therefore be treated as the final operational stage of the transaction, after the commercial terms and payment arrangements have already been confirmed.

Transaction Records

Keeping written records helps both parties understand what domain was discussed, what price was agreed and what transaction procedure was selected.

If payment instructions appear different from previously confirmed details, verify them through a trusted communication channel before sending funds.

Visitors should therefore avoid submitting confidential credentials, financial account passwords, authentication codes or other highly sensitive information through ordinary website forms or email.

Inquiry and transaction-related information may be retained for as long as reasonably necessary for communication, business records, security, dispute handling, accounting or legal obligations. Retention periods can depend on the type of information and circumstances of the interaction.
