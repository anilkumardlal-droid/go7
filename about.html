5 Pricing & Negotiation

Domain pricing can vary depending on the individual domain, its length, memorability, extension, commercial usefulness, demand and other circumstances.

An asking price or an initial offer should not be treated as the final transaction price unless the final terms have been clearly confirmed between the buyer and seller.

Serious and reasonable offers may be considered. Where negotiation takes place, the final domain name, agreed price, payment method and transfer procedure should be confirmed before payment.

6 A Simple Transaction Process

The exact process can vary depending on the domain and transaction circumstances, but a typical domain transaction may follow these steps:

Step 1 — Inquiry: The interested buyer contacts us regarding a particular domain.
Step 2 — Domain identification: The exact domain being discussed is confirmed.
Step 3 — Discussion: Availability, pricing and relevant transaction conditions are discussed.
Step 4 — Agreement: The buyer and seller confirm the exact domain, final amount, payment method and transfer procedure.
Step 5 — Payment: Payment is made using the mutually agreed method.
Step 6 — Verification: Applicable payment conditions are verified before the transfer is treated as complete.
Step 7 — Transfer: The domain is transferred through the relevant registrar or standard domain-transfer process.
Step 8 — Completion: Relevant communication and transaction records are retained by the parties.

7 Transparent Transactions

We believe important transaction details should be clear before money is sent. Buyers should know exactly which domain they are purchasing, the final agreed amount, the payment recipient and how the domain will be transferred.

If payment instructions change unexpectedly, the buyer should pause and independently verify the new information before proceeding.

Important: Always verify the exact domain name and receiving account before a transfer is initiated.
